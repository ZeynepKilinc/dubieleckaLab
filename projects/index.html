Research

Our research investigates how aging, chronic inflammation, and the bone marrow microenvironment shape blood cancer development, progression, and relapse.

Basic Science

We investigate how aging and chronic inflammation alter hematopoietic stem cells and the bone marrow niche. Our work explores the cellular interactions and signaling mechanisms that support malignant transformation and blood cancer progression.

Explore our discoveries

Translational Research

We connect discoveries in experimental models with questions relevant to human disease. Using cellular and molecular profiling, we investigate vulnerabilities in disease-initiating stem cells to inform new therapeutic strategies.

Our approaches

Clinical Research

We collaborate with clinicians to investigate how disease biology relates to patient outcomes. Our interests include biomarkers, immune alterations, and clinical factors associated with the course of hematologic malignancies.

Technologies and Approaches

From tissue to insight

Complementary experimental and computational tools help us connect the bone marrow microenvironment with cellular behavior and disease biology.

Flow Cytometry

Measuring fluorescence and light scatter to characterize immune and hematopoietic cell populations.

Mass Cytometry

Profiling multiple cellular markers using metal-tagged antibodies to investigate complex cell populations.

Spatial Transcriptomics

Examining gene expression in its tissue context to study the organization of the bone marrow niche.

Single-Cell Profiling

Exploring molecular states and heterogeneity across individual normal and malignant cells.

Bone Marrow Tissue Analysis

Examining tissue architecture and the relationships between hematopoietic, immune, and stromal compartments.

Cellular Profiling

Integrating cellular measurements to characterize disease-associated phenotypes and functional states.

Molecular Interaction Studies

Investigating protein interactions and signaling networks, including proximity proteomics approaches.
